abstract: 'The UK generated 26.4 million tonnes of household waste in 2019, corresponding to 12% of total waste generation. England was responsible for 83% of this household waste, and just 46.2% of it was recycled. Despite such large volumes of waste being produced in England, understanding how waste flows through the waste management system and the country is incomplete. Knowledge of the transportation of waste is particularly limited, especially from a greenhouse gas emissions perspective. This study seeks to answer the following questions: How and where is municipal solid waste (MSW) generated and treated in England? Which waste management strategies could minimise the environmental impacts of MSW? To this aim, mass flow data are compiled from the UK government’s “high-resolution” MSW flow data. The extended dataset is analysed through material flow analysis and life cycle assessment and visualised using Sankey diagrams to build a full picture of waste management in England. It was found that MSW generation and the greenhouse gas emissions associated with treating MSW streams are 25.1–28.5 million tonnes of MSW and 11.1 MtCO 2 e, respectively, in 2025. Furthermore, the results show significant regional variation in waste composition and waste treatment, highlighting reporting gaps and opportunities for infrastructure improvements. The findings offer a valuable tool for decision-makers seeking to optimise waste management strategies and plan new facilities.'
